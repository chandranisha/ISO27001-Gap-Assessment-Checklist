# Research Methodology

**Author:** Nisha Chandra  
**Focus:** Cybersecurity governance, risk and compliance, cloud security, and application security  
**Status:** Research approach / working document

## 1. Purpose

This document describes the approach used to plan and document independent cybersecurity research. It is intended to make future work transparent, traceable, and reproducible where practical.

## 2. Research lifecycle

### Step 1 — Define the question
- State a narrow, answerable research question.
- Explain why it matters to security practice.
- Define scope, assumptions, and exclusions before collecting evidence.

### Step 2 — Review existing evidence
- Search academic databases, standards bodies, official security guidance, and reputable technical publications.
- Record search terms, dates, inclusion criteria, and reasons for excluding relevant-looking material.
- Prioritise primary sources and peer-reviewed work where available.
- Distinguish published evidence from opinion, vendor claims, and hypotheses.

### Step 3 — Assess source quality
For each material source, record:
- Author or issuing organisation and publication date.
- Source type and review status.
- Methods and evidence supporting its claims.
- Limitations, potential bias, and relevance to the research question.

### Step 4 — Select a method
Depending on the question, methods may include:
- Structured literature review.
- Comparative framework analysis with explicit mapping criteria.
- Analysis of public, appropriately licensed datasets.
- Controlled experiments in a lab environment.
- Case-study analysis using only authorised and non-confidential information.

### Step 5 — Analyse and document
- Explain the analysis steps so another practitioner can follow them.
- Preserve relevant assumptions and record deviations from the original plan.
- Separate observations, interpretation, and recommendations.
- Avoid claiming causation from evidence that only supports correlation.

### Step 6 — Report findings
A research report should include:
1. Title and abstract.
2. Problem statement and research question.
3. Background and related work.
4. Methodology and scope.
5. Findings and supporting evidence.
6. Discussion and practical implications.
7. Limitations and threats to validity.
8. Conclusion and future work.
9. References and, where appropriate, supplementary artefacts.

## 3. Cybersecurity and publication ethics

- Test only systems owned by the researcher or covered by explicit authorisation.
- Do not publish credentials, personal data, sensitive logs, client information, employer-confidential details, or exploitable vulnerability details without appropriate permission and disclosure coordination.
- Follow applicable law, employment obligations, dataset licences, and publication policies.
- Cite sources and identify adapted material.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest.
- Label hypotheses and work in progress accurately; do not describe unreviewed work as peer-reviewed.
- Do not fabricate data, citations, experiments, or results.

## 4. Research quality checklist

Before publication, verify:
- [ ] The research question is specific.
- [ ] The scope and method are stated.
- [ ] Material claims have traceable sources or evidence.
- [ ] Relevant prior work is acknowledged.
- [ ] Findings are distinguished from assumptions and recommendations.
- [ ] Limitations are clearly stated.
- [ ] The work is original and properly attributed.
- [ ] No confidential or unauthorised data is included.
- [ ] Technical steps are safe and reproducible where appropriate.
- [ ] The target venue's submission and ethics requirements are met.
